Method note: web search only (about 18 calls), mostly abstract/listing-level. Full texts were fetched in none of the cases (Becker 2016 PDF returned 403; MBLWHOI page unreachable). Claims under "Cited Findings" are limited to what search snippets/abstracts stated. ## 1. How do papers present alongshore variability of Hs, Tp, direction, setup, IG waves and currents on reefs and beaches?

### Takeaway
Explicit multi-transect alongshore comparisons on reefs are rare; the closest are Holsclaw et al. (Maldives atoll island, 2025, three areas, setup and reef-flat currents) and Apotsos et al. (2008, Duck-type beach with canyon, 450 m alongshore). Most reef papers are single-transect (Pomeroy, Becker, Gawehn, Sous, Monismith, Cheriton multi-site but one transect each), so the alongshore dimension is presented by site-to-site comparison and by setup-gradient / momentum-balance reasoning.

### Cited Findings
- Holsclaw, Lindhart and Masselink (2025) studied alongshore variability of setup and reef-flat currents on a Maldives atoll island. Normally incident waves gave higher local setup, creating alongshore setup gradients that were the dominant driver of reef-flat currents; wave direction had minimal effect on current speed/direction; existing setup parameterizations agreed with observations in three distinct areas. — [Plymouth data record / summary](https://pearl.plymouth.ac.uk/fose-datasets/61), data DOI 10.24382/22f1a07b-e28e-4cce-9643-155cbeed1e67; journal article not located. Related: Lindhart, Masselink, Holsclaw, Scott (2026) JGR Oceans 131(2), e2025JC023063 (citation as listed in the same search; not opened).
- Holsclaw dataset instruments (inferred by the fetch tool from file names only, treat as unconfirmed): RBR Solo/Virtuoso/Duo pressure loggers, ADVs, a Solinst barometric logger, one RBR ADCP at T1, sites labeled T1-T3 and A-series, deployment about Mar-Aug 2024. This is the nearest published analogue to the 4EWA design (pressure transects + ADVs + one ADCP). — [Plymouth data record](https://pearl.plymouth.ac.uk/fose-datasets/61)
- Apotsos, Raubenheimer, Elgar, Guza (2008) JGR 113, C07025: wave height and radiation stress at the outer edge of the surf zone (depth about 2.5 m) varied by up to factors of 4 and 16 over 450 m alongshore, giving setup differences up to about 0.1 m along the shoreline; setup still dominated by the cross-shore radiation-stress gradient (1D cross-shore momentum balance worked); alongshore setup gradients can drive O(1 m/s) alongshore currents. Site is onshore of a submarine canyon (NCEX, Torrey Pines area per the MBLWHOI listing title). — [Apotsos et al. 2008, DOI 10.1029/2007JC004514](https://data-ww3.ifremer.fr/BIB/Apotsos_etal_JGR2008.pdf)
- Hansen, Raubenheimer, List, Elgar (2015) JGR Oceans 120, 1887-1903 modelled alongshore circulation and force balances at the same canyon site. — [DOI 10.1002/2014JC010555 (via CSDMS record)](https://csdms.colorado.edu/wiki/Reference:Reference-005950)
- Lowe, Falter, Monismith, Atkinson (2009) "Wave-driven circulation of a coastal reef-lagoon system" (Kaneohe Bay), JPO, DOI 10.1175/2008JPO3958.1: reef-flat currents typically <20 cm/s; lagoon setup reduced cross-reef setup gradients by 60-80%. Companion numerical paper JGR, DOI 10.1029/2008JC005081. — [JPO preprint](https://data-ww3.ifremer.fr/BIB/Lowe_etal_JPO2009.pdf)
- Monismith, Herdman, Ahmerkamp, Hench (2013) JPO 43(7), 1356-1379, DOI 10.1175/JPO-D-12-0164.1 (Moorea north shore): linear wave theory fit data even with wave height near mean depth; flux needs Stokes drift plus Eulerian transport; setup matches radiation-stress theory only after fitting two parameters (breaking, effective crest depth). — [DukeSpace](https://dukespace.lib.duke.edu/items/92f48e16-c737-40d8-9e73-1ddc56dc2f91)
- Harris, Vila-Concejo, Webster, Power (2015) Marine Geology 363, 220-229, DOI 10.1016/j.margeo.2015.02.010: spatial variation of wave transformation and sediment entrainment on a reef sand apron (details not retrieved). — [MARUM listing](https://publications.marum.de/2782)
- Cheriton, Storlazzi, Rosenberger (2020) Front. Mar. Sci. 7:351, DOI 10.3389/fmars.2020.00351: seven Pacific reef sites; larger share of energy goes to the IG band on steeper fore reefs with shallower reef flats. — [USGS](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70210915)
- Sous et al. (2019) Continental Shelf Research 184, 66-80, DOI 10.1016/j.csr.2019.07.010: cross-shore pressure array plus one velocity profiler for more than two months on a barrier reef; depth-dependent filtering of incident energy, very-low-frequency standing patterns, undular bore trains. — [Archimer](https://archimer.ifremer.fr/doc/00508/61960)
- Quataert et al. (2015) GRL 42, 6407-6415, DOI 10.1002/2015GL064861: XBeach calibrated on Roi-Namur field data; runup increases with water level and wave height. — [USGS](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70158670)

### Inferences
- A defensible 4EWA analogue to Holsclaw 2025 and Apotsos 2008 is a transect-vs-transect panel set (A-E stacked) of Hs at matched depth, setup at shoreline/reef-flat sensors, and mean-current vectors, plus an alongshore profile of setup vs. transect position (with offshore Hs and direction overlaid). Apotsos 2008 suggests the key diagnostic is whether the 1D cross-shore momentum balance explains setup at each transect, and whether residual setup differences scale with alongshore gradients in incident Hs.
- The "factor of 4 in Hs / 16 in radiation stress over 450 m" style of summary (ratio max/min across transects at a fixed depth contour) is a compact alongshore-variability metric to copy.

### Gaps
- No multi-transect alongshore reef experiment with 5 transects over about 3 km was found other than Holsclaw et al. (2025) (journal reference not located).
- No source retrieved for Lentz/Feddersen Torrey Pines alongshore array papers, Thornton/Guza alongshore array overviews, Vetter, Falter-specific array papers, or Storlazzi alongshore-array work. Search returned only older Torrey Pines linear-array reports (1977-78) and Feddersen publication index without a relevant item.

## 2. What figure types characterize swell events (cross-shore Hs/E(f), dissipation budget, transmission, Hs/h, tidal IG modulation, setup/tide regression, current vectors, directional spectra, coherence/phase, free vs bound IG, reflection, Sxy/Sxx)?

### Takeaway
The literature supports these analysis products directly: cross-shore short-wave vs IG partitioning (Pomeroy 2012, Cheriton 2020), modal/EOF and incoming-outgoing flux decomposition (Becker 2016), VLF classification (Gawehn 2016), reflection from pressure-velocity pairs (Sheremet 2002), free-vs-bound separation from arrays and bispectra, and setup vs radiation stress (Monismith 2013, Apotsos 2008). The presentation conventions (what is plotted per event) are mostly implicit in these papers and not documented in the snippets I retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- Pomeroy, Lowe, Symonds, Van Dongeren, Moore (2012) JGR 117, C11, DOI 10.1029/2012JC008310: 3-week Ningaloo study; short-wave band (0.04-0.2 Hz) dissipated on the reef crest, IG band (0.004-0.04 Hz) increasingly dominant toward the lagoon; free IG on a steep (about 1:20) fore reef generated mainly by breakpoint forcing, not shoaling bound waves; IG damped by bottom friction, at a slower rate than short waves. — [marineinfo listing](https://marineinfo.org/id/publication/355876)
- Becker, Merrifield, Yoon (2016) JGR Oceans, "Infragravity waves on fringing reefs in the tropical Pacific: Dynamic setup", DOI 10.1002/2015JC011516 (the DOI 10.1002/2015JC011651 quoted in one query is a typo): four reefs (about 135-420 m long), cross-shore pressure and currents, energetic 0.001-0.05 Hz motions; EOF analysis shows modal variability; incoming/outgoing linear-flux estimates indicate partially standing modes during energetic events; builds on Merrifield et al. (2014) forced-damped-oscillator model. — [PDF](https://data-ww3.ifremer.fr/BIB/Becker_etal_JGR2016.pdf) (opening text only via search summary)
- Gawehn, van Dongeren, van Rooijen, Storlazzi, Cheriton, Reniers (2016) JGR Oceans, DOI 10.1002/2016JC011834: about 5 months of water-level and wave data from one Roi-Namur cross-reef transect; VLF (0.001-0.005 Hz) motions sorted into resonant, standing, progressive-growing, progressive-dissipative classes depending on reef-flat depth and offshore wave period; resonance lasted about 0.5-6 h with 0.14-0.83 m heights; Hilbert-Huang Transform proposed alongside Fourier. — [USGS](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70177880)
- Pequignet, Becker, Merrifield, Aucan (2009) GRL 36, DOI 10.1029/2008GL036259: forcing of resonant modes on a fringing reef (Ipan, Guam) during tropical storm Man-Yi (details from citing works only). — [EarthRef record](https://earthref.org/ERR/121543)
- Sheremet, Guza, Elgar, Herbers (2002) JGR 107, DOI 10.1029/2001JC000970: collocated pressure and velocity sensors (1-6 m depth) decompose IG waves into shoreward and seaward components assuming cross-shore propagation; squared reflection coefficient (seaward/shoreward energy flux) varied about 0.4-1.5 cross-shore; seaward IG waves not phase-locked to incident groups, consistent with strong shoreline reflection; shoreward IG amplified offshore by nonlinear coupling with groups, coupling and flux decrease in the surf zone. — [Ifremer-hosted PDF](https://data-ww3.ifremer.fr/BIB/sheremet_jgr_part1.pdf) (abstract-level)
- Sheremet, Guza, Herbers (2005) JGR (SandyDuck): alongshore wavenumber-frequency estimator from an alongshore array of pressure and velocity sensors separates seaward from shoreward propagating waves; strong dissipation of shoreward IG across a wide range of alongshore wavenumbers suppresses edge-wave excitation. — [PDF](https://data-ww3.ifremer.fr/BIB/Sheremet_etal_JGR2005.pdf); DOI not verified.
- Herbers, Elgar, Guza (1995) JGR: 24-element coherent pressure array (13 m depth, about 2 km offshore, nine months); IG directionality tied strongly to swell direction; WKB model of free IG released in the surf zone and refractively trapped on a gently sloping shelf. — [PDF](https://data-ww3.ifremer.fr/BIB/Herbers_etal_JGR1995.pdf); DOI not verified.
- A cross-shore pressure array over a longshore bar used bispectra (short-wave/IG phase coupling) to split IG into bound and free parts; bound IG damped at the bar crest. — [search summary (source paper not identified)](https://ascelibrary.com/doi/10.1061/40549%28276%29114) (not verified; treat as pointer only)
- Torrey Pines linear-array experiments (1977-78) already used Sxy (onshore flux of longshore momentum) as the driver of longshore currents and found refraction did not produce marked alongshore breaker-height gradients in that case. — [DTIC report](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA026223.pdf), [Pawka chapter](https://www.springerprofessional.de/en/torrey-pines-experiment/13374076)
- Buckley, Lowe, Hansen, van Dongeren (2016) JPO 46, 2317-2333, DOI 10.1175/JPO-D-15-0148.1 is a flume study (not field): setup over rough vs smooth reefs; Buckley et al. (2018), DOI 10.1029/2018JC013933: roughness reduced LF motions and runup (about 30%). — [USGS data release citation](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70238000)

### Inferences
Per figure type (question answered / typical pitfall); this is my synthesis, not directly sourced beyond the studies above.
- Event-by-event cross-shore Hs and E(f) profiles (one panel set per transect per event): where does energy go, and how do swell and IG bands partition? Pitfall: pressure-to-elevation conversion with depth-dependent transfer function blows up at high frequency and for sensors near the seabed (cutoff-frequency choice must be stated); mixing different record lengths/tide stages between sensors.
- Energy-flux / dissipation budget (Ds = -dF/dx): how much is lost to breaking vs friction? Pitfall: needs consistent direction (cos theta) and group velocity in very shallow water; differentiating noisy flux between sparse sensors gives noisy Ds; reflection/IG flux ignored.
- Transmission coefficient (Hs reef flat / Hs offshore) and Hs/h: reef filtering and saturation. Pitfall: Hs/h depends on which depth (including setup) is used; offshore reference must be outside the breaker zone at that tide.
- Tidal modulation of IG (IG Hs or variance vs tide level, per transect): depth-dependent filtering and resonance (Gawehn 2016, Sous 2019, Cheriton 2020). Pitfall: tide and offshore wave height/period co-vary over a few spring-neap cycles; need to bin by both or regress jointly.
- Setup vs tide / vs Hs^2 regression: setup scaling and fit parameters (Monismith 2013, Holsclaw 2025). Pitfall: sensor datum errors and barometric correction; zero-setup reference selection; sensor drift or burial; low-pass window choice mixes VLF into setup.
- Mean current vectors and alongshore setup gradient: whether currents are driven by alongshore pressure gradients (Holsclaw 2025, Apotsos 2008). Pitfall: ADV height above bed vs profile; wave-averaged vs Lagrangian (Stokes) velocity; tilt/compass errors near steel or in shallow water; point measurements unrepresentative of reef-flat roughness.
- Directional spectra (MEM/IAE) and Sxy/Sxx: incident direction and refraction, radiation stress. Pitfall: need collocated velocity or an array of at least 3 pressure sensors with known geometry; MEM can produce spurious narrow peaks; direction convention (from/to, true vs shore-normal) confusion. Sxy sign depends on coordinate rotation per transect.
- Coherence and phase between sensors (propagation direction, free vs bound IG): coherence and phase vs frequency for sensor pairs along a transect and across transects. Pitfall: phase wrapping and spatial aliasing when separation exceeds a fraction of wavelength; coherence significance depends on degrees of freedom (state the number of averages); bound IG expected phase about 180 degrees relative to short-wave envelope (Sheremet 2002 context) but low-frequency noise and tides contaminate.
- IG reflection (Sheremet 2002 pressure-velocity decomposition or Goda/Suzuki-type 2-3 sensor method): R^2(f) per transect. Pitfall: assumes cross-shore propagation and a locally uniform depth; obliquity and edge waves bias R; sensor spacing sets singular frequencies for Goda-Suzuki (need to mask near-singular bands).
- EOF/modal analysis and flux decomposition (Becker 2016): standing vs progressive IG modes. Pitfall: requires several sensors on the reef flat; non-stationarity across tides.

## 3. Which standard QA/overview graphics support a data paper (coverage matrix, tide, wind/wave forcing, bathymetry + instrument cross-section, spectrogram per sensor)?

### Takeaway
I found datasets and cross-reef studies that make deployment documentation explicit, but no peer-reviewed guidance on a standard set of QA graphics. The recommended list below is therefore inferred from the examples found.

### Cited Findings
- USGS publishes field data releases with full instrument-metadata, e.g., UWAFRE 2012 water level and velocity measurements (Buckley et al. data release) and the associated "data plots" gallery. — [USGS UWAFRE data](https://www.usgs.gov/data/water-level-and-velocity-measurements-2012-university-western-australia-fringing-reef), [data plots](https://www.usgs.gov/media/images/data-plots-uwafre)
- Plymouth (Holsclaw et al.) archives raw logger files per instrument with naming by transect/instrument, rather than gridded products. — [Plymouth data record](https://pearl.plymouth.ac.uk/fose-datasets/61)
- Sous et al. (2019) and Cheriton et al. (2020) describe the cross-shore arrays as pressure sensors plus a velocity profiler with deployments of weeks to months (context for deployment-summary figures). — [Archimer](https://archimer.ifremer.fr/doc/00508/61960), [USGS](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70210915)

### Inferences
Suggested overview-figure list for 4EWA (question answered / pitfall); this is a design proposal, not a literature-standard.
1. Site map with transects A-E, bathymetry, sensor positions, ADCP, coordinates in a local alongshore/cross-shore frame. Q: where is everything? Pitfall: mixing datums (MSL vs chart datum vs ellipsoid) between survey and sensors.
2. Per-transect bathymetry + instrument cross-section (sensor elevation above bed, depth range, ADV height). Q: what depth range does each sensor see across tides? Pitfall: sensor burial/drift over the record.
3. Deployment coverage matrix (sensor x time, colored by data type and QC flag, with gaps/dry periods). Q: which pairs/transects overlap in time for comparisons? Pitfall: sensors drying out at low tide shown as "good" data.
4. Forcing time series: tide, offshore Hs/Tp/direction (ADCP or outermost sensors), wind, atmospheric pressure. Q: what conditions occurred; where are the events? Pitfall: wind from a distant station; barometric correction omitted for non-vented pressure sensors.
5. Spectrogram per sensor (or per transect panel stack) with tide overlay. Q: data quality, band partition, instrument noise floors, biofouling. Pitfall: different FFT settings between sensors; pressure attenuation correction amplifying noise at high f.
6. Event table + event-by-event cross-shore Hs/E(f), per transect, per band (swell/IG/VLF). Q: transformation and filtering. See Section 2 pitfalls.
7. Alongshore comparison (A-E): Hs at fixed depth, setup, mean current, IG ratio, as function of alongshore coordinate. Q: alongshore variability and its drivers (Holsclaw 2025, Apotsos 2008).
8. Cross-sensor coherence/phase and reflection maps (frequency vs cross-shore distance) per transect. Q: propagation, free vs bound, reflection (Sheremet 2002, Becker 2016).
9. Data-quality summary: clock offsets/sync check (e.g., common reference event), pressure sensor drift vs tide predictions, ADV SNR/correlation/ping count histograms. Pitfall: unsynchronised clocks create spurious phase in coherence analyses.

### Gaps
- No data-descriptor-paper template (e.g., Scientific Data, ESSD) for nearshore wave experiments was retrieved. Searching "data descriptor wave pressure sensor array nearshore" in Earth System Science Data/Scientific Data is a suggested next step.

## 4. For each figure type, the scientific question and typical pitfall

See Section 2 (analysis figures) and Section 3 (QA/overview figures) Inferences, which already pair each figure with a question and a pitfall. Additional cross-cutting pitfalls, drawn from the cited literature:
- Setup parameters must be fitted (Monismith 2013), so a "setup vs Hs^2" figure should state fitted breaking and crest-depth parameters rather than present a parameter-free comparison.
- Alongshore variations in setup can arise from offshore wave focusing (Apotsos 2008) or reef morphology (Holsclaw 2025); a transect-by-transect plot alone does not separate the two without a refraction/propagation model or offshore-forcing comparison.
- IG definitions differ between studies (e.g., Pomeroy 0.004-0.04 Hz; Becker 0.001-0.05 Hz; Gawehn VLF 0.001-0.005 Hz); state band limits explicitly and test sensitivity.
