## Q1. Which figure types recur, with paper-by-paper evidence, band definitions and nondimensional parameters?

### Takeaway
Across the field reef papers the same ~10 figure types recur: (1) cross-shore profile of SS and IG wave height or energy flux, (2) spectra or spectrograms at several cross-shore stations, (3) setup vs incident wave height colored by tide, (4) IG (or VLF) height vs SS height scatter colored by water depth, (5) wave height vs local depth with a gamma line, (6) incoming/outgoing IG flux and reflection coefficient, (7) bispectrum / biphase / nonlinear transfer vs flux divergence, (8) momentum or energy balance terms, (9) friction/dissipation vs forcing or roughness, (10) currents or circulation vs wave height. Directional-spread figures are rarer (Krier-Mariani 2022, Marques 2024, Elgar 2001/2005, Huntley 1998, Guza 2012). Most reef papers assume a shore-normal 1D transect; only Krier-Mariani 2022, Acevedo-Ramirez 2021, Marques 2024, Azouri 2024 and the Da Silva 2023 model address alongshore structure.

## Q2. What questions/hypotheses does each figure type test, and what result would one expect or look for?

### Takeaway
Each recurring figure maps to a testable statement: SS height decays by friction/breaking and is depth-limited; IG height grows through shoaling then either keeps growing (wave-like, low friction, low-slope or deep) or decays (frictional/dissipative); setup scales with offshore wave energy flux and is higher at low tide for a given wave; IG variance is tide-modulated through nonlinear loss in the surf zone (sandy beach) or through depth-dependent friction (reef flat); the IG band shows modal/standing structure only on bounded flats.

### Cited Findings
- Cross-shore SS height/flux: tests whether SS is attenuated seaward of the surf zone by friction. Marques 2024 reports consistent SS flux decay for h <= 8 m with mean Hs ~ 1 m (Fig 4) and reduction in Hs of 0.1-0.3 m (15-25%) between 21 m and 5 m (text); Acevedo-Ramirez 2021 reports energy flux dissipation up to 0.077 kW/m^2 across a spur-and-groove zone and 3x higher dissipation over spurs than grooves (Fig 4, 6). [Marques 2024](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Marques_2024_ObservationsOfWaveEnergyDissipation.pdf); [Acevedo-Ramirez 2021](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Acevedo-Ramirez_2021_WaveTransformationOnAFringing.pdf)
- SS height vs local depth: tests depth limitation; Lowe 2009 finds no waves above H_rms = 0.3 h on the reef flat (Fig 4b,c). [Lowe 2009](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Lowe_2009_WaveDrivenCirculationOfA.pdf)
- IG vs SS scatter (Becker 2016 Fig 2): tests whether IG scales with SS and whether depth organizes the scatter. Result: r^2 0.54-0.95 across sites, considerable scatter at Roi and Ipan, some depth dependence. Pomeroy 2012 Fig 4: normalized IG height increases with water depth on the reef (tide modulates it). Cheriton 2016: IG waves of ~1.0 m and VLF ~0.7 m at the shoreline during the largest event coinciding with spring high tide. [Becker 2016](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Becker_2016_InfragravityWavesOnFringingReefs.pdf); [Pomeroy 2012](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Pomeroy_2012_TheDynamicsOfInfragravityWave.pdf); [Cheriton 2016](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Cheriton_2016_ObservationsOfWaveTransformationOver.pdf)
- Source of IG (bound vs breakpoint): tested with cross-correlation of the SS envelope with the IG time series vs spatial lag against the shallow-water speed line (Pomeroy Fig 10, Becker 2016 Fig 8, Buckley 2018 Fig 5, Masselink 2019 Fig 5-6). Pomeroy 2012 finds breakpoint forcing dominated on a 1:20 fore reef; Masselink 2019 gives slope thresholds (> 1/10 breakpoint-forced, < 1/20 bound-wave release matters). [Pomeroy 2012](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Pomeroy_2012_TheDynamicsOfInfragravityWave.pdf); [Masselink 2019](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Masselink_2019_PhysicalAndNumericalModelingOf.pdf)
- Incoming/outgoing IG flux and reflection coefficient: tests shoaling gain, surf-zone loss and shoreline reflection. Sheremet 2002: shoreward IG flux grows seaward of the surf zone, drops inside it, R^2 spans 0.4-1.5; Pequignet 2014: IG flux increases on the reef face then decreases through the surf zone, with the incoming IG decrease attributed to breaking SS waves working against bound IG; fIG flux increases through the surf zone (breakpoint forcing). [Sheremet 2002](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Sheremet_2002_ObservationsOfNearshoreInfragravityWaves.pdf); [Pequignet 2014](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Pequignet_2014_EnergyTransferBetweenWindWaves.pdf)
- Bispectrum/biphase/bicoherence and nonlinear transfer: tests nonlinear coupling and bound IG. Elgar 1985: biphases of ~Stokes-like type at 9 m evolving to shoreward-pitched shapes; Pequignet 2014 Fig 7-8: difference interactions (zone A) with biphase near 180 deg (consistent with bound waves) on the reef face; Henderson 2006 and Thomson 2006: nonlinear transfers roughly balance IG flux divergence within ~150 m of shore; adding bottom drag with Cd > ~1e-2 degraded the balance (Henderson). [Elgar 1985](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Elgar_1985_ObservationsOfBispectraOfShoaling.pdf); [Henderson 2006](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Henderson_2006_NonlinearGenerationAndLossOf.pdf); [Thomson 2006](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Thomson_2006_TidalModulationOfInfragravityWaves.pdf)
- Tidal modulation: on a sandy beach, IG variance at low tide is ~1/4 that at high tide because nonlinear surf-zone loss is stronger over the convex low-tide profile (Thomson 2006: nonlinear transfers explain > 70% of the modulation). On reefs: setup is larger at low tide for a given incident wave (Becker 2014, abstract; Beetham 2016 abstract), SS height on the flat is depth-limited, IG height rises with reef-flat depth (Pomeroy 2012) but Beetham 2016 reports no tidal modulation of IG on the Funafuti flat; Merrifield 2014: setup, SS and IG each show a tidal dependence but the 2% exceedance level sums to a tide-independent value. [Thomson 2006](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Thomson_2006_TidalModulationOfInfragravityWaves.pdf); [Becker 2014](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Becker_2014_WaterLevelEffectsOnBreaking.pdf); [Beetham 2016](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Beetham_2016_WaveTransformationAndShorelineWater.pdf); [Merrifield 2014](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Merrifield_2014_ObservationsAndEstimatesOfWaveDriven.pdf)
- Setup vs offshore wave: tests radiation-stress dynamics. Vetter 2010: reef-flat setup ~35% of incident H_rms in 8 m (r > 0.95), up to 1.3 m during Man-Yi; Lowe 2009: setup roughly proportional to offshore wave energy flux above a threshold Hrms ~0.7 m (Fig 8b uses Hrms,0^2*Tp); Tait 1972 estimate ~20%; Raubenheimer 2001: setup well predicted in outer/mid surf zone but underpredicted near the shoreline, and increases with decreasing surf-zone-averaged slope. [Vetter 2010](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Vetter_2010_WaveSetupOverAPacific.pdf); [Raubenheimer 2001](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Raubenheimer_2001_FieldObservationsOfWaveDriven.pdf)
- Setdown offshore of the surf zone: tests the Longuet-Higgins and Stewart relation. Raubenheimer 2001: setdown predicted well from linear-theory radiation stress at each location, and by analytical prediction from offshore waves; Vetter 2010 Fig 6 compares observed setdown with the LH and Stewart 1962 equation; Buckley 2015: underprediction without wave roller. [Raubenheimer 2001](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Raubenheimer_2001_FieldObservationsOfWaveDriven.pdf); [Buckley 2015](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Buckley_2015_DynamicsOfWaveSetupOver.pdf)
- Momentum balance / currents: tests whether radiation-stress gradient is balanced by pressure gradient (beach-like) or by friction (open-channel-like). Sous 2020: transition from beach-like on the fore reef to friction-dominated across the barrier; setup on the reef top depends on depth; mean Cd 18e-3. Lowe 2009: reef-flat currents increase ~linearly with incident wave height but weak (typically < 20 cm/s), lagoon setup reduces cross-reef gradient by 60-80%. Symonds 1995: subtidal cross-reef currents correlated with offshore wave height; spectral peak at twice the tidal frequency in the currents. Hearn 1999: current depends on depth over reef with a maximum at some depth. [Sous 2020](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Sous_2020_MomentumBalanceAcrossABarrier.pdf); [Lowe 2009](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Lowe_2009_WaveDrivenCirculationOfA.pdf); [Symonds 1995](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Symonds_1995_WaveDrivenFlowOverShallow.pdf)
- Dissipation/friction: tests whether energy flux convergence matches U_rms^3 (bottom friction). Marques 2024: r^2 = 0.91 for one pair; hourly f_e between 1 and 10 (abstract), increasing as A_b/sigma_h decreases, power-law in binned means; Acevedo-Ramirez 2021: f_w = 1.01 explains dissipation over spurs (R^2 = 0.9). Gerritsen 1980: breaking-zone friction coefficients 0.1-0.5. [Marques 2024](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Marques_2024_ObservationsOfWaveEnergyDissipation.pdf)
- Modal/resonance: tests whether IG/VLF on a flat is a standing mode: EOF spatial structure vs cos(k_n x) and spectral peaks at f_n = (2n+1) sqrt(g h)/(4L) (Pequignet 2009, Becker 2016 Fig 4-6, Buckley 2018 Fig 6-9, Gawehn 2016 Fig 2-5). Gawehn: four VLF classes set by reef-flat depth and incident-band period. Edge/leaky waves: Sheremet 2002 Fig 9 and Azouri 2024 Fig 13 use f-k_y spectra with edge-wave dispersion curves (Azouri: eastward-propagating 0.5-2 min IG in the high-mode-edge/leaky regime in models). [Gawehn 2016](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Gawehn_2016_IdentificationAndClassificationOfVery.pdf); [Azouri 2024](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Azouri_2024_ComputationsOfEnergeticNearshoreWaves.pdf)
- Directional spread: Krier-Mariani 2022 shows that oblique and reflected energy dominate the inner platform (onshore transect-aligned waves = 36% of SW and 22% of IG energy), so 1D flux assumptions can fail; Guza 2012: realistic directional spread changes modeled IG runup by a factor equivalent to a factor-two change in incident wave height; Marques 2024 Fig 9 compares total-flux gradient vs cross-shore-flux gradient from directional moments (a1, b1). [Krier-Mariani 2022](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Krier-Mariani_2022_PerspectivesOnWaveTransformationOver.pdf); [Guza 2012](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Guza_2012_EffectOfWaveFrequencyAnd.pdf)

### Inferences
- For the three largest swell events, the literature suggests looking for: SS Hs decreasing shoreward along each transect and a normalized flux F/F0 decreasing even at 8-10 m if the bed is rough (Marques); IG height increasing shoreward (Sheremet, Pequignet 2014, Pomeroy) and IG/SS ratio rising with depth or falling tide-dependently; low-tide vs high-tide splits of IG that differ if the Thomson-type surf-zone mechanism acts (low-tide IG reduced) vs the reef-flat friction mechanism (IG increases with depth). These are hypotheses, not 4EWA results.
- If transects differ (A-E), the ratio of IG/SS or f_e across transects tests alongshore variability in bed roughness or refraction (Acevedo-Ramirez spur/groove contrast and Marques roughness dependence are the precedent); the Library gives no predicted magnitude for Ewa.
- Evidence for "free vs bound IG" at 10 m depth would be SS-envelope/IG cross-correlation peaking at zero lag (bound, negative correlation per Longuet-Higgins and Stewart) vs lagged; Pomeroy Fig 10 and Masselink Fig 5-6 show the display. The expected sign/lag was not verified in the captions and should be checked in those papers before use.

### Gaps
- Expected IG/SS ratios or setup percentages at a ~1.3 km offshore, ~10 m sensor line are not available: the cited numbers (e.g. 35% setup, IG 20-30% of incident height at best in Masselink 2019 model) are from steep reefs, reef flats or platforms.
- No Library paper tests tidal modulation of IG at 10 m depth on a reef shelf; Thomson 2006 is a sandy-beach result and Pomeroy/Beetham results are reef-flat results that conflict (IG tide-modulated at Ningaloo, not at Funafuti).
- The expected sign/lag of bound-wave envelope correlation was not extracted from any paper's text.

## Q3. Which processing choices matter for each figure?

### Takeaway
The processing choices that most change a figure are: the SS/IG split frequency and upper cutoff; whether pressure is converted to surface elevation with linear theory (SS band, with a cutoff at ~0.2 Hz at depth) or left as is for IG; how setup is referenced (offshore sensor with relative-drift removal and setdown correction, or a remote still-water level); averaging length for means (15-30 min); the shallow-water-speed assumption in incoming/outgoing separation; instrument QC for ADVs (SNR, correlation, pressure-velocity z^2 and coherence); and an alongshore-offset criterion when using pairs for flux gradients.

### Cited Findings
- Frequency band: choose and state; see Q1 band list. Pomeroy 2012 used a per-burst f_split = half the offshore peak frequency (mean 0.04 Hz), consistent with Roelvink and Stive 1989; Cheriton 2016 notes no natural gap between IG and VLF and limited VLF resolution. Henderson 2006 shows results can be evaluated for sub-bands (0.005-0.025, 0.025-0.05 Hz). [Pomeroy 2012](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Pomeroy_2012_TheDynamicsOfInfragravityWave.pdf); [Cheriton 2016](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Cheriton_2016_ObservationsOfWaveTransformationOver.pdf); [Henderson 2006](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Henderson_2006_NonlinearGenerationAndLossOf.pdf)
- Pressure to surface elevation: Pomeroy 2012 applied linear wave theory to the short-period waves but not to IG (shallow even at 15.7 m); Cheriton 2016 applied pressure-response corrections; Becker 2014 depth-corrected detided 15 min subrecords using linear theory, with acoustic surface tracking at the 13.8 m offshore AWAC; Vetter 2010 found the Bernoulli correction negligible (< 2%) and temperature-related error < 0.2%; Marques 2024 limits SS to 0.2 Hz because noise overwhelms the small pressure signal at higher frequencies and recomputed with local vs spatially averaged depth (results within ~20%). [Pomeroy 2012](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Pomeroy_2012_TheDynamicsOfInfragravityWave.pdf); [Becker 2014](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Becker_2014_WaterLevelEffectsOnBreaking.pdf); [Vetter 2010](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Vetter_2010_WaveSetupOverAPacific.pdf); [Marques 2024](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Marques_2024_ObservationsOfWaveEnergyDissipation.pdf)
- Setup referencing: Vetter 2010 computes setup from 15 min mean water levels (30 min gives similar results; averaging filters IG), referenced to the offshore 8 m sensor, assuming its setdown is negligible; Becker 2014 eq. (3): eta_i = h_i - h_f - (b t + c) + setdown_f, where b and c remove relative pressure drift and set the offset so that setup is zero when the excess reef-face wave height H_f - 1.2 H_i = 0 (differs from Vetter's zero at H_f = 0 because of larger tides at Roi/CMI), and eq. (4) setdown = -H^2 k/(8 sinh(2kh)); Becker 2014 used Paroscientific sensors for setup because they drift less than piezoelectric sensors; Cheriton 2016 applies the same b t + c fit and normalizes to the offshore setdown. Sous 2020 computed setup as the 40-min average level minus a still-water level from a gauge ~9 km away. [Vetter 2010](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Vetter_2010_WaveSetupOverAPacific.pdf); [Becker 2014](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Becker_2014_WaterLevelEffectsOnBreaking.pdf); [Cheriton 2016](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Cheriton_2016_ObservationsOfWaveTransformationOver.pdf); [Sous 2020](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Sous_2020_MomentumBalanceAcrossABarrier.pdf)
- Atmospheric and shallow-depth exclusions: Vetter 2010 corrected for atmospheric pressure with a land barometer and excluded emerged-sensor times; Becker 2014 excluded < 0.1 m of water; Cheriton 2016 required reef-flat depth >= 0.2 m; Marques 2024 excluded h < 2 m (rock outcropping) and Hs/h >= 0.25 for friction estimates. [Vetter 2010](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Vetter_2010_WaveSetupOverAPacific.pdf)
- Incoming/outgoing separation: Pomeroy 2012 and Sheremet 2002 use collocated elevation and velocity with the shallow-water speed sqrt(g h) for IG (Guza et al. 1984 method), compute linear fluxes (nonlinear contribution ~15% of linear on the flat), and note that the AWAC near-surface velocity was too noisy so an 8 h running mean was applied to F_IG at C1; reflection R^2 = F-/F+ ; Goda 1976 is the two-gauge alternative. [Pomeroy 2012](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Pomeroy_2012_TheDynamicsOfInfragravityWave.pdf); [Goda 1976](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Goda_1976_EstimationOfIncidentAndReflected.pdf)

[Marques 2024](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Marques_2024_ObservationsOfWaveEnergyDissipation.pdf)

[Thomson 2006](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Thomson_2006_TidalModulationOfInfragravityWaves.pdf); [Elgar 1985](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Elgar_1985_ObservationsOfBispectraOfShoaling.pdf)

### Inferences
- At ~10 m depth the pressure-to-elevation transfer function strongly attenuates f > ~0.2 Hz; following Marques 2024 an SS upper cutoff near 0.2 Hz is a safe default, and ADV velocity can serve as the cross-check for the z^2 test.
- Setup referencing: 4EWA's Paros sensors and the Honolulu NOAA gauge allow either Becker-style offshore-sensor referencing (offshore sensor, drift fit b*t + c, excess-height offset, setdown term) or a Sous-style remote still-water level. Becker 2014's preference for Paroscientific sensors supports using the Paros subset for setup.
- If 4EWA has no shallow reef-flat sensor, the setup figure should be framed as setdown/setup between array stations (Raubenheimer 2001 approach, with a radiation-stress prediction from the local H and k) rather than as reef-flat setup.
- For pairwise flux gradients, a 1.3 km cross-shore, 3 km alongshore array will produce many pairs with large dy/dx; the Marques filter (|dy/dx| < tan 30 deg, 20 <= dx < 120 m) would remove most of them, so spacing must be checked against the actual sensor coordinates.

### Gaps
- Library papers do not say how to correct pressure-derived elevations for 10 m sensors at the SS/IG boundary specifically (Pomeroy's fore-reef C1 used an AWAC surface track).
- Mean-flow (setup) averaging length for a 2D array at ~10 m: 15-40 min is used in the cited papers for 1-3 h bursts; the literature does not test sensitivity at these depths.
- Bernoulli/velocity-head and wave-current effects on pressure-derived mean level were quantified only by Vetter 2010 (< 2%) and referenced via Raubenheimer et al. 1998 in Elgar 2005's text; not re-derived here.
