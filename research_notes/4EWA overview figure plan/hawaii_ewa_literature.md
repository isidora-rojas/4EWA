## Summer wave climate, sheltering and refraction at Ewa / Mamala Bay

### Takeaway
Summer (about May-Sep) is the south-swell season, with trade-wind seas present year-round. Quantitative Tp, Hs and direction statistics for Ewa were not found in the search. The best next step is to compute them directly from the CDIP 238 (Kalaeloa) record.

### Cited Findings
- Kalaeloa shoreline (south coast, transects 336-538) is carbonate sand over limestone with a fringing offshore reef. Waves: persistent trade-wind waves year-round, southerly swell in summer, and refracted westerly swell in winter. Underlying limestone is intermittently exposed or buried by shifting sand. — [UH Coastal Research, Kalaeloa to Kualaka'i](https://www.soest.hawaii.edu/crc/?p=707) (search summary; page not fetched)
- Aucan (2006) climatology identifies three swell sources for Oahu: boreal-winter NW swell, trade-wind seas, and boreal-summer S swell. Trade-wind seas come from about 60-90 deg (NNE-E) and are notably constant in Jun-Sep. NW swell is nearly absent Jun-Sep at Waimea. The analysis used Waimea and Mokapu buoys only, so there is no Ewa-specific result. — [Aucan 2006 workshop presentation](https://www.waveworkshop.org/conferences/2006/Presentations/Aucan.pdf)
- South-shore season is May-Sep. Sources are Southern Hemisphere storms in the South Pacific (New Zealand-Antarctic sector), travelling about 4000-6000 miles, giving long-period, well-organized swell with gaps between sets. A typical face height of about 5.5 ft is attributed to the UH Sea Level Center surf climatology. This is a face height, not Hs, and it is a non-scientific secondary source. — [hawaii-guide.com](https://www.hawaii-guide.com/blog/hawaii-south-shore-surf-season) (low reliability)
- UHSLC hosts Oahu surf climatology products, including a "South Shore annual cycle" plot and Buoy 51026 wind statistics. These are a lead for a climatology panel. — [UHSLC quick links](https://uhslc.soest.hawaii.edu/outreach/climo/quick_links.html)
- Vitousek & Fletcher (2008), Pacific Science 62(4):541-553: the maximum annually recurring Hs for open N/NW Pacific swell is 7.7 m. A USGS table citing the paper gives about 1.6 m (Hs) and 2.7 m (1%) for the 210-240 deg sector. This is a useful bound that summer south-swell Hs is small relative to winter. — [DOI 10.2984/1534-6188(2008)62[541:MARWHI]2.0.CO;2](https://doi.org/10.2984/1534-6188(2008)62[541:MARWHI]2.0.CO;2)
- Island blocking (Maui sources): a straight south swell works better than a southwest swell because of shadowing by neighboring islands. This was not verified for Oahu or Ewa, but it is relevant to the Ewa swell window (Niihau/Kauai shadow of SW-W swell, and Waianae coast shelter to the NW). — [Maui Now](https://mauinow.com/tag/storms-swells/) (secondary, UNVERIFIED for Oahu)

### Inferences
- Ewa faces S to SSW, and the Waianae range shelters it from NW swell and trade-wind seas to some degree. Expect bimodal spectra: S swell at about 14-20 s (to be checked against CDIP 238) plus short-period wind sea. (Inference from general knowledge, not from the cited sources.)
- Overview figure candidates: Hs/Tp/Dp time series from CDIP 238 over the deployment, a directional spectrogram, and wind-sea versus swell partition.

### Gaps
- No Hs, Tp or direction statistics for the Ewa/Kalaeloa buoy in summer were found. Paper candidates not located: Merrifield et al. 2014 (Oahu reefs), Becker et al. on Hawaii wave climate, and any Mamala Bay refraction modeling study.
- Direct numbers for Mamala Bay sheltering and refraction were not found.

## Wave transformation, setup, infragravity waves and currents on Oahu / Hawaiian reefs

### Takeaway
Hawaiian reef field studies exist, but none were found at Ewa. The closest analogs are Kaneohe Bay (Lowe et al. 2009), Mokuleia (Vetter 2007), and Becker et al. 2016 on tropical Pacific fringing reefs. Hanalei and Molokai (USGS) provide dataset and figure precedents.

### Cited Findings
- Lowe et al. (2009, JPO), Kaneohe Bay: a 10-month experiment showed that wave forcing dominates circulation. Setup near the reef crest drives flow across the reef and out through reef channels. Setup scales with offshore wave energy flux above Hrms of about 0.7 m, and reef-flat currents are typically below 20 cm/s. — [Lowe et al. 2009 PDF](https://data-ww3.ifremer.fr/BIB/Lowe_etal_JPO2009.pdf) (search summary)
- Vetter (2007) UH MS thesis: Mokuleia, Oahu has a reef about 100 m wide, rougher and deeper (about 1.2 m) than Ipan, Guam. Setup at Mokuleia is about 3 times lower for the same incident wave height. The setup balance is the radiation stress gradient against the pressure gradient. — [Vetter 2007](https://www.soest.hawaii.edu/oceanography/masters/2007-Vetter.PDF)
- Becker, Merrifield & Yoon (2016, JGR), "Infragravity waves on fringing reefs in the tropical Pacific: Dynamic setup": four reefs, 135-420 m long. Low-frequency motions are modal and partially standing. A one-parameter linear model explains 75-97% of the variance of low-frequency Hs. The sites are not confirmed to include Hawaii. — [PDF](https://data-ww3.ifremer.fr/BIB/Becker_etal_JGR2016.pdf) (search summary; fetch blocked)
- Waimanalo thesis (UH, 2024): non-hydrostatic XBeach validated with pressure transducers at two nearshore sites. IG waves were present at both sites. — search result (title: "Nonhydrostatic XBeach Simulation of Wave Transformations in a Fringing Reef Environment"; URL not recorded, UNVERIFIED)
- USGS Molokai data (Waiakane, 2018): sensors from offshore to the inner reef flat over 86 days. — [ScienceBase](https://www.sciencebase.gov/catalog/item/5d7ab280e4b0c4f70d02a48a)
- Azouri, Roeber & Luther (2018), UH Oceanography seminar abstract: North Shore Oahu IG response is dominated by modes with periods of 1 to nearly 30 min. — [UH calendar PDF](https://www.hawaii.edu/site/calendar/uploads/34220.pdf) (abstract only)
- Kauai Hanalei Bay: Hoeke, Storlazzi & Ridd (2013, Cont. Shelf Res.) wave-flow coupled model, and a 2011 JGR paper on wave climate and observations. — [USGS-related search result](https://pubs.usgs.gov/metrics/publication/70199974) (UNVERIFIED link relevance)
- Storlazzi, Cheriton, Messina & Biggs (2018, Coral Reefs): circulation and residence time in Faga'alu Bay, American Samoa. This is not Hawaii, but it is a good precedent for current-roses and residence-time figures. — search result (no URL retained)

### Inferences
- Standard figure vocabulary in these papers: cross-shore bathymetry profile with instrument positions, Hs/Tp offshore versus reef-flat time series, setup versus offshore wave power scatter, IG/SS spectra by station, and current roses or vectors.

### Gaps
- No published wave or current study at Ewa, Kalaeloa, Pearl Harbor or Hawaii Kai was found. None for Waikiki hydrodynamics was found either. No sediment/coral dynamics papers were located.
- Merrifield 2014, Cheriton Oahu and Storlazzi Oahu papers were not found, so their existence is unconfirmed.

## Reef geometry at Ewa

### Takeaway
No quantitative Ewa reef-flat width, slope or channel data were found. Qualitative: carbonate sand over limestone with a fringing reef and shifting sand cover.

### Cited Findings
- Kalaeloa (White Plains/Nimitz) shoreline shows alternating erosion and accretion cells, with maximum erosion of about -0.9 ft/yr near transect 439 and maximum accretion of about 0.5 ft/yr near transect 373. Barbers Point Beach is roughly stable to accreting. — [UH Coastal Research](https://www.soest.hawaii.edu/crc/?p=707)
- The Kalaeloa Hybrid Reef Project filing (2026) describes concrete structures in 7-13 ft (about 2-4 m) water depth offshore of Campbell Industrial Park, modeled on a reef crest and back reef. The application is pending. — [DLNR K-2](https://dlnr.hawaii.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/K-2.pdf)
- Barbers Point deep outfall monitoring stations BP-1 to BP-3 lie inshore of the outfall, about 1.6-2.9 km from the terminus. — [NCEI accession 0000174](https://ncei.noaa.gov/archive/accession/0000174)
- Barbers Point area description exists, but the fetch failed. — [UH SOEST](https://ftp.soest.hawaii.edu/coastal/webftp/Oahu/Online/Descriptions/BarbersPtAreaDescription.htm)

### Inferences
- Alongshore variability of exposed limestone versus sand, and any reef channels, would modulate setup and currents. Mapping from USGS/PacIOOS/NOAA lidar bathymetry (not searched) is recommended for an overview map.

### Gaps
- Reef-flat width, crest depth, fore-reef slope, and channel positions were not found.

## Tides and existing instruments / datasets

### Takeaway
Tide is mixed, small and well documented. CDIP 238 (PacIOOS/NDBC 51212) off Kalaeloa is the obvious wave comparison dataset, and its predecessor 165 covers 2010-2017.

### Cited Findings
- NOAA Honolulu 1612340 datums (metric, epoch 1983-2001, from the NOAA API): MHHW 1.741, MHW 1.600, MSL 1.412, MLW 1.211, MLLW 1.161. Great Diurnal Range 0.58 m, Mean Range 0.389 m, DHQ 0.141 m, DLQ 0.05 m. This gives about 0.58 m MLLW-to-MHHW with a clear diurnal inequality. — [NOAA MDAPI](https://api.tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov/mdapi/prod/webapi/stations/1612340/datums.json?units=metric)
- PacIOOS wave buoy CDIP 238 / NDBC 51212: moored in 291 m of water, about 2.75 km offshore of Kalaeloa Harbor, Datawell Directional Waverider (replaced with a DRW4 on 2022-03-02), 30-min data. It also provides surface current and temperature observations. — [PacIOOS](https://www.pacioos.hawaii.edu/waves/buoy-kalaeloa/)
- Predecessor Barbers Point buoy CDIP 165 / NDBC 51204 operated 2010-10-11 to 2017-02-19. — [Data.gov](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/pacioos-wave-buoy-165-barbers-point-oahu-hawaii1) (via search summary)
- Mamala Bay circulation: only advocacy and old (1960s) current-meter and drogue documents were found. — [CZIC report](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CZIC-qe500-h35-no-64-1/html/CZIC-qe500-h35-no-64-1.htm)

### Inferences
- Tidal range of about 0.6 m is comparable to reef-flat water depth, so tidal modulation of wave transmission and setup should be shown. Plot tide against Hs on the reef.
- Whether the user's own deployment has a modest net tidal current depends on local data, which is not covered here.

### Gaps
- Sand Island/Mamala Bay circulation studies (ADCP) were not found, and no Ewa-reef instrument deployments were found. The NOAA tide type label was not retrieved directly (inferred from DHQ and the great diurnal range).
