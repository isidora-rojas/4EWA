# Ten figures that will frame 4EWA

The strongest next figures are cross-shore profiles of swell-band (SS) and infragravity-band (IG) wave height for each transect and each of the three big events. After that come an IG-versus-SS scatter coloured by depth, an alongshore comparison at fixed depth, and a coverage/instrument-geometry sheet that all later figures depend on. Nearly every precedent in the user's Library puts these first: Pomeroy 2012, Cheriton 2016, Marques 2024 and Acevedo-Ramirez 2021 all open with a cross-shore Hs or flux profile, and Becker 2016 opens with the IG-versus-SS scatter. Those four figures need only the hourly `*_spec.nc` files, which already exist for all 14 pressure sensors. A second tier covers transect spectra, tidal modulation, buoy-versus-array direction and mean currents. These are feasible now but need caveats. Setup, reflection, bispectra and any cross-shore distance or shoaling prediction are deferred. The Paros air offset is unresolved (about 0.1 m), the loose-prong ADVs make direction doubtful, and no bathymetry or shoreline file exists. The literature offers no Ewa-specific field study, and the evidence is mostly caption-level. Reef and platform papers dominate, so no number from them should be taken as an expectation for a 4 to 10 m, 1.3 km shelf. The array's alongshore extent (five transects over about 3 km) is where it can add something the 1D transect papers lack.

## The literature supports a standard figure set but not an Ewa prediction

Across the field reef papers in the Library, about ten figure types recur: cross-shore SS and IG profiles, spectra at several stations, setup versus incident height coloured by tide, IG versus SS scatter, height versus depth with a gamma line, incoming/outgoing IG flux, bispectra, momentum or energy budgets, friction versus forcing, and currents versus wave height. Directional or alongshore figures are rarer ([Library inventory, notes Q1](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library)). Only Krier-Mariani 2022, Acevedo-Ramirez 2021, Marques 2024 and Azouri 2024 address alongshore structure ([Krier-Mariani 2022](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Krier-Mariani_2022_PerspectivesOnWaveTransformationOver.pdf); [Acevedo-Ramirez 2021](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Acevedo-Ramirez_2021_WaveTransformationOnAFringing.pdf); [Marques 2024](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Marques_2024_ObservationsOfWaveEnergyDissipation.pdf); [Azouri 2024](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Azouri_2024_ComputationsOfEnergeticNearshoreWaves.pdf)). The closest design analogue found outside the Library is Holsclaw, Lindhart and Masselink (2025) on a Maldives island. It used pressure loggers on transects, ADVs and one ADCP to study alongshore setup variation and reef-flat currents ([Plymouth dataset record](https://pearl.plymouth.ac.uk/fose-datasets/61)). Its journal article was not located, and the instrument list there was inferred from file names. Apotsos et al. (2008) supplies the alongshore-variability template: wave height and radiation stress at about 2.5 m depth varied by factors of up to 4 and 16 over 450 m alongshore, yet the 1D cross-shore momentum balance still held ([Apotsos 2008](https://data-ww3.ifremer.fr/BIB/Apotsos_etal_JGR2008.pdf)).

The gaps matter as much as the precedents. No field study of Ewa, Kalaeloa or Mamala Bay hydrodynamics turned up, and no reef-flat width, crest depth or channel data. The Library cannot say whether a reef flat or shallow terrace lies shoreward of the array. The nearest Oahu analogues are Lowe et al. 2009 at Kaneohe Bay, which is a lagoon-reef system ([Lowe 2009](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Lowe_2009_WaveDrivenCirculationOfA.pdf)), and Azouri 2024 on the north shore, which is model-based. The only Ewa-adjacent descriptions are qualitative: carbonate sand over limestone with a fringing reef and shifting sand cover ([UH Coastal Research](https://www.soest.hawaii.edu/crc/?p=707)). The substantive consequence is that the figures below are framed as tests of hypotheses, and no expected magnitude is borrowed from steep fore-reef papers. The Library notes say outright that IG/SS ratios and setup percentages at a roughly 10 m sensor line are unavailable. The quoted 35% setup (Vetter 2010) and IG fractions come from reef flats and platforms.

The existing project figures also shape the plan. `figs/initial/A1_overview.png` already stacks buoy Hs/Tp/Dp, Honolulu tide, SS Hs of the nine ADVs, subtidal speed and wind, with event windows shaded. `Hs_SS_vs_IG_*.png`, `bulk_transect{A..E}_*.png`, `Hs_bands_*.png`, `A2_spectrogram*.png` and `spectrum_p_*.png` also exist. The audit viewed only a few of these. Descriptions of the others rest on file names and notebook code and are unverified ([project audit, section 5](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/2026/4EWA/research_notes/4EWA%20overview%20figure%20plan/project_audit.md)). The user's existing sensor map, Hs/Tp/Dmean series and spectra are therefore treated as done. Where a recommended figure overlaps an existing one, the plan says to upgrade it rather than start over.

## Four first-tier figures can be built from files already on disk

The ranking below weighs three things: how many Library papers open with the figure, how much of the array it uses, and whether the data are trustworthy today. All depth values are the pressure-derived medians from the audit (ADVs 4.2 to 11.0 m, Paros 2.3 to 3.0 m).

**Figure 1. Event-resolved cross-shore profiles of SS and IG Hs, one panel per transect.**

*Purpose.* This is the core "what happens to the waves between 10 m and 2 to 3 m" figure.

*Design.* The x-axis is local mean depth, measured from pressure, with a secondary axis of relative along-transect distance (sensor to sensor, not from shore; the audit states that no shoreline or bathymetry exists). The y-axis is Hs in metres, with a second row showing Hs normalised by the 10 m value, in the manner of Marques's F/F0. Use one column each for transects B, C, D and E. Transect A has a single sensor (VA10) and should appear only as an extra point on the 10 m line. One row per event: 24 July, 16 August and 8 September, taking the event peak hour plus a pre-event calm hour. Draw SS as filled markers and IG as open markers. Use a continuous depth ordering so that the tide-driven depth changes within an event can be shown as a shaded range or by two tide stages. Include the Paros (PB1, PB2, PC1, PD1, PE1) only for the events after 28 July (PB/PC) or 5 August (PD/PE), which excludes the 24 July event.

*Questions.* Does SS Hs decay shoreward at every transect, and where does the decay begin? The 8 September numbers already show strong attenuation: 4.4 to 5.4 m at 10 m against 2.2 to 3.2 m at 5 m and 1.1 to 1.4 m at the Paros ([audit section 4](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/2026/4EWA/research_notes/4EWA%20overview%20figure%20plan/project_audit.md)). Does IG Hs grow shoreward in the opposite sense? Median IG Hs rises from 0.05 to 0.07 m at the 10 m ADVs to 0.08 to 0.135 m at 5 m and 0.16 to 0.21 m at the Paros (same source), which is the sign Sheremet 2002 and Pomeroy 2012 report for IG approaching a surf zone. Do transects differ at matched depth, and is the difference tied to event direction? A related question is whether Hs/h at 10 m is already near breaking in the 8 September event. From the audit figures, 5.4 m at about 10.4 m gives Hs/h of roughly 0.5. This is my own arithmetic, not in the notes. It would put the 10 m line inside or near the breaker zone and make Marques's "not breaking" criterion of Hs/h below 0.25 inapplicable for that event.

*Precedent.* Pomeroy 2012 Fig 3 to 4 (burst-averaged short-wave and IG H_rms across stations, with IG normalised by fore-reef height); Cheriton 2016 Fig 4 (cross-reef profiles per band, split by low/high water and large events); Marques 2024 Fig 4 (Hs and normalised flux versus mean depth with 25th/75th percentile bars); Acevedo-Ramirez 2021 Fig 4 (energy flux, percent dissipation) ([Pomeroy 2012](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Pomeroy_2012_TheDynamicsOfInfragravityWave.pdf); [Cheriton 2016](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Cheriton_2016_ObservationsOfWaveTransformationOver.pdf)). Marques's reported 0.1 to 0.3 m (15 to 25%) Hs reduction between 21 and 5 m for Hs of about 1 m gives a reference for the calm-day panels only.

*Feasibility and caveats.* Hs is stored for 0.04 to 0.25 Hz. IG is computed from `Seta` over 0.004 to 0.04 Hz and is not stored. The Hs upper cutoff of 0.25 Hz exceeds Marques's 0.2 Hz, and the cosh(kh) correction amplifies noise above about 0.3 Hz at 10 m. Test a 0.2 Hz cutoff before publication. The hourly Welch estimate uses 512 s segments (df 0.00195 Hz), so the IG lower edge at 0.004 Hz sits on only about two spectral bins. This is my inference from the stated settings, so the IG band's low edge is poorly resolved. State the band, as literature definitions differ (Pomeroy 0.004 to 0.04 Hz; Becker 0.001 to 0.05 Hz; Pequignet 0.005 to 0.05 Hz). Paros have no z2 test, and the hourly `seg_ok` for them checks only completeness. The 16.5 s gap each hour is harmless for IG at these segment lengths. Mean depth differs by tide, so the depth axis needs the tide stage encoded. Existing `Hs_bands_*.png` show the band split but not versus position, so this figure is new, not a duplicate.

**Figure 2. IG-versus-SS scatter coloured by local depth, all pressure sensors.**

*Purpose.* It tests whether IG scales with SS energy and whether depth organises the scatter.

*Design.* The x-axis is Hs,SS and the y-axis is Hs,IG, both on log axes. Use one panel per depth group (10 m, 5 m including VE4 and VE7, and Paros) with 14 sensors in all. Colour by instantaneous depth, which includes tide, rather than by date. Overlay a small marker for each of the three events, and add a straight-line reference through the 10 m panel.

*Questions.* Is IG roughly proportional to SS (the 8-panel existing figure is near-linear), and does the slope change shoreward? Becker 2016 Fig 2 reports r² of 0.54 to 0.95 across four reefs with some depth dependence, and Pomeroy 2012 Fig 4 shows IG height normalised by fore-reef SS height rising with water depth ([Becker 2016](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Becker_2016_InfragravityWavesOnFringingReefs.pdf)). The late-July cluster of high-IG points at VA10, VC10 and VB5 in the existing figure raises a specific question: is that the 24 July long-period (20 s, 0.05 Hz) swell, whose peak sits close to the SS lower edge (0.04 Hz in the project definition, 0.05 Hz in several papers)? Whether band-edge choice moves this swell energy between SS and IG is a hypothesis to test, not a finding.

*Precedent.* Becker 2016 Fig 2; Pomeroy 2012 Fig 4; Cheriton 2016 Fig 3. *Caveat and improvement.* `Hs_SS_vs_IG_2026-07-19_2026-09-16.png` plots only the eight 10 m and 5 m ADVs, coloured by date. Improve it by adding the Paros and VE7, colouring by depth and tide, and splitting by Tp class. This is an upgrade of an existing figure, not a new one. Maximum IG Hs reaches 0.77 to 0.80 m at VA10 and VB5, which is large against a 0.05 to 0.07 m median and deserves an event check.

**Figure 3. Alongshore comparison at fixed depth lines, with offshore forcing overlaid.**

*Purpose.* This is the figure that justifies a 2D array. It asks whether the 10 m and 5 m lines see the same waves from A to E.

*Design.* The x-axis is the approximate alongshore position (A at about -1250 m, B -690 to -570, C -180, D +1150, E +1810 m, from a projection on 80 degrees true; the audit warns this is approximate because shoreline headings vary from 66 to 93 degrees). The y-axis is event-peak Hs, IG Hs and Dm in separate stacked panels. Draw two lines: the 10 m line (VA10, VC10, VD10, VE10) and the 5 m line (VB5, VC5, VD5, VE4). Add the buoy value as a horizontal reference. Use a line plot or small multiples, not a heatmap, because only four or five alongshore positions exist at each depth. Report the max/min ratio across transects, as Apotsos did.

*Questions.* How large is the alongshore spread of Hs at the 10 m line in each event? In the 8 September event the 10 m values run VA10 5.44, VD10 5.01, VE10 4.60 and VC10 4.38 m, a spread of about 25%. Are the highest values at the western end (A) consistently, and does that match the direction of approach? Does the alongshore pattern at 5 m resemble the 10 m pattern, or is it reorganised by local depth? The 24 July event is the reverse: VC10 2.43, VA10 2.28, VE10 1.51 and VD10 1.47 m, so it is not simply a gradient. Does the pattern track bed roughness (the Acevedo-Ramirez spur/groove contrast, three times higher dissipation over spurs) or wave focusing (Apotsos)? A transect plot alone cannot separate them without a refraction model or an offshore forcing comparison ([notes, alongshore section 4](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/2026/4EWA/research_notes/4EWA%20overview%20figure%20plan/alongshore_array_literature.md)).

*Precedent.* Apotsos et al. 2008 (alongshore variation of Hs and radiation stress over 450 m); Holsclaw et al. 2025 (three alongshore areas); Acevedo-Ramirez 2021 Fig 6 (spur versus groove). Figure numbers for Holsclaw and Apotsos were not verified.

*Caveats.* Alongshore sampling is coarse (four to five points), spacing is irregular (A-B 560 m, B-C 505, C-D 970, D-E 665 m), and the "5 m line" is really 4.2 to 5.3 m deep. Normalise by depth or compare at matched depth before reading an alongshore gradient. Transect A has no 5 m or Paros sensor. VB5 and VE4 have loose prongs, but their pressure-derived Hs is not affected by the loose head in the way direction is. This distinction is not documented in the audit, so confirm it before relying on VB5 and VE4 Hs.

*Design.* Left panel, a sensor-by-time matrix (15 rows, grouped by transect, with the ADCP shown as cells 1 to 13) coloured by `seg_ok` and by data type, with the three event windows overlaid. Right panel, for each transect, a schematic of depth against relative distance with the ADV and Paros positions and median depths. Mark the loose-prong sensors and the ones with offset issues.

*Precedent.* Pequignet 2014 Fig 1 and Lowe 2009 Fig 2 (map with bathymetry and instruments); the proposed QA list in the alongshore notes (coverage matrix, per-transect cross-section) is a design proposal, not a literature standard ([notes, Section 3](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/2026/4EWA/research_notes/4EWA%20overview%20figure%20plan/alongshore_array_literature.md)).

The KML in the repo root carries the old IDs, a lidar line scan footprint and "White Plains topo points", but no data. The README says 23 ADCP cells and the QC file has 19, of which 13 are usable (1.1 to 7.1 m above the transducer). Reconcile this before plotting. ## Second-tier figures are feasible now but need explicit qualification

**Figure 5. Along-transect spectra for the three events.** The purpose is to show where energy moves between bands. Plot variance-preserving spectra (f·S against log f), one panel per transect, with the 10 m, 5 m and Paros curves at the event peak hour and the SS/IG band edges drawn as vertical lines. Questions: does energy shift to low frequency shoreward, as in Cheriton 2016 Fig 2 and Pequignet 2014 Fig 3? Is the 24 July 20 s swell visible as a distinct peak against the 16 August 8.5 to 9.5 s peak? Does a second-harmonic or sea-wind peak appear? Precedent: Pequignet 2014 Fig 3, Cheriton 2016 Fig 2, Azouri 2024 Figs 3 to 8. The user already has whole-record and event spectra for all sensors, so this should be a transect-grouped redraw, not a new calculation. State the dof (hourly averages of 512 s segments) so coherence or band claims can be judged, as Pomeroy (39 dof) does. Do not apply the pressure-to-elevation transfer function above about 0.2 Hz at 10 m.

**Figure 6. Tidal modulation of SS and IG.** Plot SS and IG Hs (or the IG/SS ratio) against tide level, binned by offshore Hs class, for the 5 m and Paros sensors. The reference tide is Honolulu (about 25 km east, lag within 4 min, r = 0.98 for Paros), and the tide's great diurnal range of 0.58 m ([NOAA datums](https://api.tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov/mdapi/prod/webapi/stations/1612340/datums.json?units=metric)) is comparable to the 2.3 to 3 m Paros depth, so the effect should be visible there. The literature gives conflicting expectations that make this a real test: Thomson 2006 reports IG variance at low tide about one quarter of that at high tide on a sandy beach, Pomeroy 2012 reports IG height rising with reef-flat depth, and Beetham 2016 reports no tidal modulation of IG on the Funafuti flat ([Thomson 2006](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Thomson_2006_TidalModulationOfInfragravityWaves.pdf); [Beetham 2016](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Beetham_2016_WaveTransformationAndShorelineWater.pdf)). No Library paper tests it at 10 m on a reef shelf. Pitfall (from the alongshore notes, my synthesis): tide and wave height covary across a few spring-neap cycles, so bin by both or regress jointly.

**Figure 7. Buoy-versus-array direction and period.** This is the refraction and sheltering figure. Plot Dm (and Dp) at each ADV against the buoy Dp for the whole record, and a frequency-resolved direction (from `Co_pE`, `Co_pN`, `Suu`, `Svv`) at the event peaks. The existing `bulk_transect*.png` already show Dm of 150 to 185 degrees at VC10 and VC5 against 175 to 210 degrees at the buoy. This offset is a headline observation, and refraction is the likely cause, but this is unverified. The 16 August event needs special treatment. The buoy gives Hs 2.3 m, Tp 12.5 s and 198 degrees, while the 10 m sensors give Hs 2.9 to 3.3 m (larger than the buoy), Tp 8.5 to 9.5 s and 141 to 153 degrees. The array peak is therefore probably a different wave train, perhaps a local or wind-driven sea (Honolulu wind peaks near 10 m/s about 16 August), and not the buoy swell. That is unverified. Show the event in wind-sea/swell partition form before calling it a swell event. The same difference in Hs for 24 July (2.4 m at the array against 1.7 m at the buoy) also needs explaining. Direction caveats are strong: only nine ADVs give direction, VB5, VE4 and VE7 have loose prongs (VE4's mean rotated direction is 10.9 degrees against 353.0 degrees at VE10, and the VE4 heading correction is not applied in `initial_figs.ipynb`), and Paros have none. Dm integrates 0.04 to 0.25 Hz, so it mixes swell and sea.

The buoy's identity also needs checking. The notebook calls it "Barbers Point", but the Hawaii notes identify CDIP 238 (NDBC 51212) as the Kalaeloa buoy in 291 m water, about 2.75 km offshore, and say the Barbers Point predecessor (CDIP 165) ran only from 2010 to February 2017 ([PacIOOS](https://www.pacioos.hawaii.edu/waves/buoy-kalaeloa/)). A 2026 record is therefore Kalaeloa, so the label is probably stale (this is my reading of the notes). The buoy location is not stored in the project.

**Figure 8. Mean-current maps and roses.** Plot subtidal velocity vectors at the nine ADVs and the ADCP on the sensor map, for a calm period, each event and the difference, plus a vertical profile of the ADCP's 13 usable cells. This matches Lowe 2009 Fig 5 to 6 (mean vectors, ellipses, subtidal current versus Hrms,0) and Vetter 2010 Fig 10, and the notebook already has B.3b (event, calm, difference), whose saved state is unverified. Upgrade that rather than duplicate it. Lowe reports reef-flat currents typically below 20 cm/s; ADV velocities in the project run from -0.75 to +0.62 m/s, so check that the colour scale resolves weak means. Caveats: VB5, VE4 and VE7 direction is suspect, the Honolulu tide gauge is distant, and ADV current should not be read as depth-mean. The ADCP's cells 14 to 19 are masked by the surface and the wave-averaged versus Lagrangian distinction matters (Stokes drift).

## Setup, reflection and cross-sensor coherence should wait for bathymetry and offsets

Several figures that dominate the Library are poorly supported by the current data, and the plan should say so before they are attempted. **Setup versus Hs² or Hs·Tp, coloured by tide** (Lowe 2009 Fig 8; Becker 2014 Fig 7; Sous 2020 Fig 9) is the most-cited result in the papers, but it is unreliable here. The Paros air offset was not applied (`qc_air_offset_applied` = 0), with pre-deployment offsets of -0.04 to -0.06 dbar and post-recovery offsets of +0.06 to +0.09 dbar, so absolute mean level is uncertain by about 0.1 m. That is the same size as the expected signal. The audit estimates 0.05 to 0.3 m at 2 to 3 m depth for Hs of 2 to 5 m, a figure that is not tested. VA10 and VE10 carry additional offset drifts of 0.31 and 0.19 dbar (0.2 to 0.3 m). The only defensible version is a change relative to a calm baseline per sensor, as a setdown/setup between stations in the manner of Raubenheimer 2001 and not as reef-flat setup, and with Becker's drift fit (b·t + c) and setdown term ([Becker 2014](file:///Users/isidorarojas/Desktop/DIRECTORY/Library/Becker_2014_WaterLevelEffectsOnBreaking.pdf)). Becker's preference for Paroscientific sensors for setup supports using the Paros subset, but their offset is the problem.

**Incoming/outgoing IG flux and reflection** (Sheremet 2002 Figs 2 to 3; Pomeroy 2012 Fig 6) need collocated pressure and velocity, and the ADVs provide them, but the method assumes cross-shore propagation and depth near h, and the notes give no 4EWA test. **Bispectra and biphase** (Elgar 1985; Pequignet 2014 Figs 7 to 8) would show whether IG at 10 m is bound, and the envelope-IG cross-correlation (Pomeroy Fig 10; Masselink 2019 Fig 5) is a cheaper version, but the expected sign and lag were not verified in any caption and should be checked first. **Coherence and phase between sensors** needs clocks aligned to a few seconds, which has been verified only for PE1-VE4 and ADCP-VC10, and the Paros hourly gap complicates cross-spectra. **Frequency-wavenumber alongshore spectra** (Sheremet 2002 Fig 9; Azouri 2024 Fig 13) need a regular alongshore array, which the irregular 560 to 970 m spacing is not. **Reef-resonance EOFs** (Pequignet 2009; Becker 2016 Figs 4 to 6) assume a bounded flat that nobody has shown exists here. Any shoaling-theory curve, slope parameter, surf-similarity or Ursell number needs the missing bathymetry. Cheriton 2016's U = Hs·λ²/h³ and Marques's friction-factor figure (f_e versus A_b/σ_h, needing bed roughness) fall in the same class. Marques's pair filters (20 to 120 m separation, |dy/dx| below tan 30 degrees) would reject most pairs in this array, since cross-shore spacings are 294 to 824 m, so a dissipation figure from sensor pairs is not feasible as designed (my inference from the stated criteria and the audit separations).

Evidence quality varies and should be carried into any figure caption or text. Everything about Library papers is caption-level or text-level only: the notes extracted text and captions with pdftotext, viewed no figure images except Elgar 2005 and 1993, and flag that Marques 2024 and Azouri 2024 figure numbers follow preprint numbering. All Hawaii and alongshore-array items came from search snippets and abstracts, with several fetches blocked. Specific unverified items: the Holsclaw 2025 article (only the dataset record was found, and its instrument list is inferred); the Lindhart et al. 2026 citation (JGR Oceans 131, e2025JC023063, not opened); Becker 2016's inclusion of Hawaii (not confirmed from the sources; the Library notes list its sites as Roi-Namur, Majuro-type Pacific reefs, Ipan and Saipan); the Waimanalo XBeach thesis (no URL recorded); Storlazzi et al. 2018 on Faga'alu Bay (no URL recorded); the Hanalei Bay papers (link relevance unconfirmed); the Maui source on island shadowing of south swell (secondary and not verified for Oahu); and the hawaii-guide.com "5.5 ft" face height (low reliability, a face height and not Hs). Goda 1976, Guza, Lygre and Krogstad, and Kuik are cited from memory in the notes only. Monismith et al. 2013 (the user's earlier-noted setup paper) is not in the Library, and the memory note that it is the likely source for the η = aH₀^b·L₀^c form is itself unconfirmed. Its own record shows only that the setup fit needed two parameters ([DukeSpace](https://dukespace.lib.duke.edu/items/92f48e16-c737-40d8-9e73-1ddc56dc2f91)). The summer wave climate for Ewa (a south-swell season from May to September, with trade-wind seas from about 60 to 90 degrees constant in June to September) comes from Aucan 2006, which used only Waimea and Mokapu buoys ([Aucan 2006](https://www.waveworkshop.org/conferences/2006/Presentations/Aucan.pdf)), and no Ewa-specific statistics were found. The best fix is to compute Hs, Tp and direction statistics directly from the CDIP 238 record already on disk. Three event names (Fausto, Lala, Lowell) come from the notebook and were not confirmed as storm names.

Finally, the event table itself deserves a small figure or table at the top of the set, since every other figure refers to it. The three events are 24 July (buoy Hs 1.67 m, Tp 20 s, 191 degrees), 16 August (2.28 m, 12.5 s, 198 degrees) and 8 September (7.21 m smoothed, 13.3 s, 240 degrees). They are ranked by buoy peak, so the ordering by Hs hides that the first two are modest and the third is exceptional. Add the 4 September event (1.75 m, 10.5 s) as a candidate fourth if a mid-size case is wanted. The 8 September buoy direction of 240 degrees is far from the south-swell pattern of the other two and raises a sheltering question (island shadowing of south-west swell is plausible but unverified for Ewa).

## Conclusion

The plan's main change to the existing set is a shift from time series to space: the user already has the time-domain overview, and the literature's most common first figures are profiles and scatters that put sensors on a depth or position axis. Because Hs/h at 10 m may already be near 0.5 in the 8 September event, the 10 m line cannot be treated as a "pre-breaking" reference in that event, which changes how normalisations and the alongshore figure should be read. The 16 August event is the clearest candidate for an early mistake: if it is shown as a swell event on the strength of buoy Hs, the sensor Tp and direction mismatch will mislead.

Three data fixes would unlock most of the deferred figures: apply or bound the Paros air offset, apply the VE4 heading correction and settle the loose-prong question for VB5, VE4 and VE7, and add lidar or topographic bathymetry. Without bathymetry, the project cannot place the array relative to the shore, the reef or any shoaling prediction, and the literature cannot say whether an Ewa reef flat exists to make the setup and resonance figures meaningful. If a reef flat is absent, the best framing for the set is the Raubenheimer and Marques one, in which the array measures shoaling, dissipation and alongshore variability on an open shelf, with the 3 km alongshore span as its distinctive contribution.
